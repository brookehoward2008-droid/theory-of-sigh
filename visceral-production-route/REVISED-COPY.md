## I. Agency

**p8 — Section opener**
- Subtitle: `The Body / presence before permission`
> Agency is the body as its own first statement: a hand, an eye, a turned face that claims
> attention as pressure, before any rule arrives to explain it.

**p9 — Article**
> The body is the first instrument of authorship, long before it becomes a subject for
> interpretation. A hand, a shoulder, a mouth, a face turning away: the figure enters as
> pressure, not as explanation. It claims the page simply by being present, and that presence
> unsettles a viewer who has not yet been handed a rule for reading it. This is the oldest
> grammar of looking. In LeRoy McDermott's study of Upper Paleolithic figurines, the strange
> proportions of the earliest carved bodies read not as another's gaze but as
> self-representation — the body seen from within, by the one who has to live inside it.[1]
> Sight begins as ownership, and only later learns to perform.
- Caption (A18 / Raw Agency): `The body speaks first, and waits for permission after.`

**p10 — Article**
> A figure can be partial and still be in command. A cropped body still claims its space; a
> single eye still returns the look. So the opening pages stay close, image-led, deliberately
> a little uncomfortable — presence arriving ahead of permission. Looking here is not passive
> reception. The eye learns by pressure, repetition, and contrast, and a body met across a
> sequence becomes a pattern the viewer is slowly trained to recognize. Before culture can
> explain the figure, the figure has already insisted on being seen.
- Caption (A24 / Raw Agency): `Sight folds inward and goes quiet.`

## II. Constraint

**p17 — Section opener**
- Subtitle: `The Rule / visibility as protocol`
> Constraint is culture turning visibility into protocol: pose, costume, rank, and ritual teach
> a body how it may appear — and teach the viewer how to approve.

**p18 — Article**
> Culture turns visibility into a protocol. Bodily force is no longer allowed to stand alone; it
> is arranged by posture, costume, rank, ritual, motherhood, and inherited rules of display. A
> face still looks outward, but now it looks through an architecture of expectation. Elizabeth
> Mulley's study of Laura Muntz gives that constraint an intimate register — womanhood rendered
> through maternity, care, loss, and symbolic burden, the body made legible by the roles it is
> asked to carry.[2] Mary Morrissy's account of Una Watters adds the ordinary: a woman set
> inside weather, labour, and the street rather than idealised apart from it.[3] Constraint does
> not erase agency. It points it somewhere.
- Caption (A02 / Social Constraint): `The gaze glitches, and survives the glitch.`

**p19 — Article**
> The body still carries force, but that force is shaped by who is permitted to look and who is
> expected to be seen. The viewer is disciplined too. Each repeated crop, pose, and symbol
> teaches a visual habit, until seeing is no longer simple contact but compliance, resistance,
> and learned interpretation at once. The room has rules, and the eye has already agreed to
> most of them before it knows it is choosing to obey.
- Caption (A04 / Social Constraint): `The eye is edited out of its own portrait.`

## III. Mediation

**p27 — Section opener**
- Subtitle: `The Veil / the tempo of access`
> Mediation is the veil as an editing system: lace, shadow, fabric, and blur do not simply hide
> the body — they decide how slowly it is allowed to be seen.

**p28 — Article**
> The veil is an editing system, not a disappearance. Where the body and the rule meet a surface
> that can interrupt both — lace, shadow, fabric, blur, flowers, hair, darkness — that surface
> becomes an interface. It does not erase the figure; it sets the rate at which the figure
> arrives. A blocked face raises the attention it denies, because the viewer is made to finish
> the missing information. Refusal becomes structure. This is what the art of obstruction has
> always known: Symbolism treats the visible world as a carrier for inward states, and
> Surrealism turns ordinary surfaces into dream-pressure and interruption.[4][5] The covered
> eye and the displaced face push the viewer past recognition, into interpretation.
- Caption (A01 / Mediation): `The veil doesn't hide the face — it schedules it.`

**p29 — Article** *(removed the "this section opens its grid" meta-line)*
> Here the body wants to appear, the rule wants to organise appearance, and the veil controls
> the tempo of access. The aim is not mystery for its own sake but cognitive pressure: a hidden
> gaze makes the eye work, and meaning arrives only through the effort. What is withheld is not
> absence. It is the part of the image still being decided.
- Caption (A20 / Mediation): `The gaze hides in the green and watches from there.`

## IV. Synthesis

**p39 — Section opener**
- Subtitle: `Sight that refuses to settle`
> Synthesis is sight that refuses to settle: body, rule, and veil stay active at once, so
> looking stays unfinished and the image keeps its pressure.

**p40 — Article** (headline: `Unresolved Sight`)
> Sight becomes visceral when these forces stay active together. The final movement refuses to
> resolve the body, the rule, and the veil into a clean hierarchy. Agency opens the argument,
> constraint disciplines it, mediation keeps it unfinished — and the image grows powerful
> precisely because no single force wins. This is the thesis the whole issue has built toward:
> psychological pressure does not come from clear depiction. It comes from calculated
> revelation — the image negotiating what can be seen, how quickly, and what stays withheld even
> after attention has been spent.
- Caption (A01 / Mediation): `Beauty was the alibi all along.`

**p41 — Article** (headline: `Looking never arrives clean.`)
> The body is present but never fully available. Culture is legible but never neutral. The veil
> interrupts, yet it also teaches the eye how to keep going. So the closing pages stay
> asymmetrical: large images take authority, text presses beside them, slightly displaced. A
> symmetrical page would claim that sight had settled — and this argument needs sight to stay
> unstable, because instability is where looking turns into learning. The anatomy of looking is
> never finished. It only changes the surface it has to cross next, and asks the eye to begin
> again.
- Caption (A02 / Social Constraint): `The glitch holds; the gaze keeps its nerve.`
